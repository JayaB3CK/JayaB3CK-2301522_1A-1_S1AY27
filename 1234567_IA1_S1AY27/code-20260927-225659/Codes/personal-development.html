Personal Development

Reflections from The Richest Man in Babylon by George S. Clason: the Seven Cures for a Lean Purse and the Five Laws of Gold.

Reading: The Richest Man in Babylon

The Seven Cures for a Lean Purse

1. Start thy purse to fattening

I try to save a small portion of whatever I receive, even when the amount is small. Building the habit matters more to me right now than the amount I save.

2. Control thy expenditures

I budget my expenses so that food and transportation fees for school are covered first, before my "wants"but don't need.

3. Make thy gold multiply

Learning web and animation skills is my way of investing in myself for future work and career opportunities.

4. Guard thy treasures from loss

I avoid quick-money schemes and rely on oneself- doing my coursework, finishing my degree, and steady skill-building fills my path towards success.

5. Make of thy dwelling a profitable investment

My room is my study corner so I try to spend less time doom-scrolling and spend my time finishing labs and assignments on time.

6. Insure a future income

I focus on finishing my degree with strong results and building a small portfolio, including this website, for future jobs.

7. Increase thy ability to earn

I practise coding, complete my labs, and keep my GitHub active to sharpen the skills I'll be paid for later.

The Five Laws of Gold

Law 1: Gold comes to those who save

If I spend everything I earn, I have no options when something important comes up. Saving puts me in control.

Law 2: Gold works for the wise owner

I only put money where I can understand what is happening to it, so I am not blindly trusting something I do not understand.

Law 3: Gold stays with the cautious

I research carefully before I buy an item, an electronic device, or any tool meant to help my studies.

Law 4: Gold slips away from the get-rich-quick

If a deal sounds too easy, it usually is. I remind myself of this when I see offers online.

Law 5: Gold flees from impossible earnings

I do not force money into anything I do not understand. I focus on what I can learn well: school, then skills, then a job.
